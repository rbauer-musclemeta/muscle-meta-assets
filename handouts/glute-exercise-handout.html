Glute training handout

Small Steps. Exponential Gains.™

Your glutes are the large muscles around your hips. They help you stand up from a chair, climb stairs, walk steadily and keep your hips and knees comfortable. This handout gives you 40 exercises to choose from, a simple way to plan each week, and a tracker to tick off your sets, all at your pace and on your terms.

Before you begin

How to use this handout

The rule of thirds: 3 days a week, 4 movement families

Train on 3 days a week, for example Monday, Wednesday and Friday. In each session, pick one exercise from each of the four families below. Change your picks from session to session so your hips are challenged in different ways.

Thrust or bridgepick one
Squat or lungepick one
Hingepick one
Abductionpick one

Effort guide: leave a few reps in the tank

At Foundation, stop each set with 3 to 4 reps still in reserve. That means you could have done 3 or 4 more with good form.

Example: 10 reps done well, 3 or 4 more possible

At Build and Advance you may work closer to hard, but keep a clear margin and never train to the point of failure on hinge exercises.

Pre-session checklist

How do I feel today?

While you train

Listen to your body

Some effort is expected. Use this guide during every session to decide whether to carry on, adjust or stop.

Carry on

  • Warm, tired glutes and thighs
  • A mild stretch behind your thighs
  • Gentle muscle soreness that eases in a day or two
  • Working hard but still in control

Adjust

  • A pinch at the front of your hip
  • A mild joint ache
  • Form slipping as you tire
  • Feeling wobbly

Try: an easier version, a smaller range, more support, a lighter load or a longer rest. If it keeps happening, mention it to your clinician.

Stop and seek advice

  • Sharp or catching joint pain
  • Chest pain
  • Dizziness
  • Unusual breathlessness
  • Numbness or tingling
  • Pain after a fall

How to read each exercise page

  • Core idea. One sentence on what the exercise is for.
  • Easy instructions. Up to 3 short steps.
  • Key cues. Two reminders to keep in mind while you move.
  • Make it easier or harder. Move up or down to suit your day.
  • Joint note. Anything to watch for with your knees, hips or back.
  • Checklist and sets. Tick the boxes before you start, and tick a circle after each set.

Thrust / bridge

Lying on your back and pressing your hips up. The floor or bench supports you, so balance and joints are barely challenged, and you recover quickly.

Foundation

  • Banded glute bridge
  • Bench hip thrust (bodyweight)
Build
  • Barbell hip thrust with pause
  • Single-leg glute bridge
  • Barbell glute bridge (floor)
Advance
  • B-stance hip thrust

Glute bridge

Thrust / bridgeNo equipment
Foundation

Core idea: Lie on your back and lift your hips, so your glutes do the work while the floor supports you.

Easy instructions

  1. Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat and hip-width apart, heels under your knees.
  2. Press through your whole foot and lift your hips until shoulders, hips and knees make one line.
  3. Pause for a second at the top, then lower over three seconds.

Key cues

  • Ribs down, tailbone tucked slightly
  • Push the floor away through your heels

Make it easier: Shorten the range and use a pillow under your hips for a gentler start.

Make it harder: Hold a dumbbell on your hips, add a band above the knees, or move to the single-leg bridge.

Joint note: Usually well tolerated by knees and hips. After a hip or knee replacement, follow your surgeon's precautions.

Banded glute bridge

Thrust / bridgeMini band
Foundation

Core idea: The same bridge with a small band above your knees, which also wakes up the muscles at the side of your hips.

Easy instructions

  1. Place a mini band just above your knees and set up as for the glute bridge.
  2. Keep gentle outward pressure into the band throughout.
  3. Lift, pause one second, lower slowly.

Key cues

  • Knees track over the second toe
  • Spread the floor apart with your feet

Make it easier: Use a lighter band, or no band.

Make it harder: Add load across the hips, or pause for three seconds at the top.

Joint note: Same joint profile as the floor bridge. Stop if the outer hip becomes tender.

Bench hip thrust (bodyweight)

Thrust / bridgeSturdy bench or sofa
Foundation

Core idea: A bridge with your shoulders resting on a bench, so your hips travel through a longer range.

Easy instructions

  1. Sit on the floor with your upper back against the edge of a stable bench.
  2. Feet flat, knees bent. Drive your hips up until your torso is level.
  3. Pause, then lower until your hips nearly touch the floor.

Key cues

  • Chin tucked, eyes forward
  • Shins close to vertical at the top

Make it easier: Use a lower surface or return to the floor bridge.

Make it harder: Hold a dumbbell across the hips and add a two-second pause.

Joint note: Gentle on knees. Getting down to and up from the floor may be the real challenge; use a higher surface if needed.

Barbell hip thrust with pause

Thrust / bridgeBarbell, Smith machine or plate-loaded machine
Build

Core idea: A loaded version of the bench thrust, where you pause at the top to feel your glutes working.

Easy instructions

  1. Sit with your upper back on a bench and the bar padded across your hips.
  2. Drive up through your feet until your torso is level. Hold two seconds.
  3. Lower under control and keep tension on the glutes.

Key cues

  • Ribs over hips at the top
  • Squeeze, then count "one-Mississippi, two-Mississippi"

Make it easier: Drop to the dumbbell version.

Make it harder: Add pause time, add load slowly, or move to the B-stance or single-leg version.

Joint note: Puts the work through your hips with little load on your spine. Breathe out as you lift, and avoid holding your breath on heavy sets.

Single-leg glute bridge

Thrust / bridgeNo equipment
Build

Core idea: A bridge on one leg, which trains your glutes to keep your hips level.

Easy instructions

  1. Set up for a glute bridge, then extend one leg so the thigh lines up with the other thigh.
  2. Lift through the planted heel, keeping hips level.
  3. Lower under control. Complete all reps, then switch.

Key cues

  • Level hips, as if balancing a tray
  • Push through the planted heel

Make it easier: Rest the toes of your free foot lightly on the floor.

Make it harder: Add a weight on the hips or elevate the shoulders.

Joint note: Gentle on the joints. A good check on side-to-side strength differences.

Barbell glute bridge (floor)

Thrust / bridgeBarbell or heavy dumbbell
Build

Core idea: A short, heavier bridge from the floor, with a squeeze at the top.

Easy instructions

  1. Lie on the floor with the load padded across your hips.
  2. Drive up to full hip extension. Squeeze at the top.
  3. Lower until the weight lightly touches the floor.

Key cues

  • Finish tall, not arched
  • Keep tension between reps

Make it easier: Use a lighter dumbbell.

Make it harder: Pause at the top or shift to the bench hip thrust for more range.

Joint note: Floor start and short range keep knees and spine calm.

B-stance hip thrust

Thrust / bridgeBench, barbell or dumbbell
Advance

Core idea: A hip thrust that works mostly one leg, with the other foot resting lightly for balance.

Easy instructions

  1. Set up for a hip thrust. Place the second foot a half-step back with only the toes touching down.
  2. Drive mostly through the front foot.
  3. Lower slowly. Complete reps on one side, then switch.

Key cues

  • Most of the push comes from the front foot
  • Keep your hips level throughout

Make it easier: Use the two-leg version.

Make it harder: Progress to the single-leg thrust.

Joint note: Similar to the standard hip thrust, with more balance demand.

Single-leg hip thrust

Thrust / bridgeBench
Advance

Core idea: The most demanding thrust, where one leg does all the lifting.

Easy instructions

  1. Set up with your upper back on a bench and one foot planted.
  2. Extend the free leg and drive the hips up.
  3. Pause, lower, complete reps and switch.

Key cues

  • Pelvis level
  • Press through the whole foot

Make it easier: B-stance version.

Make it harder: Hold a dumbbell on the working hip.

Joint note: Balance and control demand are higher. Use a wall behind the bench.

Squat / lunge

Bending your knees and hips to sit down and stand up, or stepping up and back. This is the closest match to getting out of a chair and using stairs.

Build

  • Goblet squat
  • Leg press, feet high and wide
  • Single-leg leg press
  • Reverse lunge (Smith machine or rail)
  • Walking lunge
Advance
  • High-bar squat to parallel

Chair sit-to-stand

Squat / lungeSturdy chair
Foundation

Core idea: Standing up from and sitting down on a chair, the movement you use many times a day, trained on purpose.

Easy instructions

  1. Sit near the front edge of a sturdy chair, feet slightly behind your knees.
  2. Lean forward from the hips until your nose is over your toes, then stand up.
  3. Sit back slowly over three seconds. Lightly touch the seat and go again.

Key cues

  • Nose over toes
  • Drive the floor away, then stand tall

Make it easier: Use a higher seat or a cushion, or keep your hands on your thighs.

Make it harder: Use a lower seat, hold a weight at your chest, or pause halfway down.

Joint note: If knees or hips complain, raise the seat. Rising and lowering slowly beats adding depth.

Step-up with handrail

Squat / lungeLow step, handrail
Foundation

Core idea: Step up onto a low step with a rail beside you, direct practice for stairs.

Easy instructions

  1. Stand facing a low step (4 to 6 inches to start) with a hand lightly on a rail.
  2. Place your whole foot on the step and stand up through that leg.
  3. Lower slowly with control. Do all reps, then switch.

Key cues

  • Whole foot on the step
  • Lower for three seconds

Make it easier: Lower step, use two hands.

Make it harder: Higher step, then dumbbells.

Joint note: Pick a step height that gives no knee pain. Leaning your body forward a little moves the work to your hip.

Supported split squat

Squat / lungeCounter or rail for support
Foundation

Core idea: Stand with one foot forward and one back, holding a counter, and bend both knees.

Easy instructions

  1. Hold a counter or rail. Step one foot back into a staggered stance.
  2. Lower the back knee straight down toward the floor.
  3. Press through the front foot to stand. Do all reps, then switch.

Key cues

  • Stand tall, or lean slightly forward to feel your hip more
  • Front heel stays down

Make it easier: Reduce the depth.

Make it harder: Hold dumbbells or move to the Bulgarian split squat.

Joint note: A longer step and a slight lean forward move the work toward your hip and away from your knee.

Goblet box squat

Squat / lungeDumbbell or kettlebell, box or bench
Build

Core idea: Hold a weight at your chest and sit back onto a box, which gives you the same depth every time.

Easy instructions

  1. Hold a weight at your chest and stand in front of a box set at about knee height or slightly lower.
  2. Sit your hips back and down until you lightly touch the box.
  3. Stand by driving your feet into the floor.

Key cues

  • Elbows pointing down
  • Sit between your hips, not behind them

Make it easier: Raise the box or return to the chair sit-to-stand.

Make it harder: Lower the box toward parallel or progress to the goblet squat.

Joint note: The box limits depth so you can stay in a comfortable hip and knee range.

Goblet squat

Squat / lungeDumbbell or kettlebell
Build

Core idea: A squat holding a weight at your chest, going down only as far as feels comfortable.

Easy instructions

  1. Hold a weight at your chest, feet shoulder-width, toes slightly out.
  2. Lower until your thighs are about parallel or as deep as is comfortable.
  3. Stand up through your whole foot.

Key cues

  • Chest tall, knees track over toes
  • Take your time at the bottom

Make it easier: Use a box to set depth.

Make it harder: Pause at the bottom or progress to the barbell squat.

Joint note: Depth is yours to choose. Stay in a range that is pain-free.

Leg press, feet high and wide

Squat / lungeLeg press machine
Build

Core idea: Press a platform away from you on a machine that supports your back, with your feet placed high and wide.

Easy instructions

  1. Sit with your back flat on the pad and your feet high and slightly wide on the platform.
  2. Lower until your knees are bent about 90 degrees, stopping before your hips lift off the seat.
  3. Press back without locking your knees.

Key cues

  • Keep your hips glued to the seat
  • Press through the heels and mid-foot

Make it easier: Lighter load, shorter range.

Make it harder: Single-leg leg press or a slow three-second lowering phase.

Joint note: Supported and predictable. Usually friendlier than free squats for knees and balance.

Single-leg leg press

Squat / lungeLeg press machine
Build

Core idea: Press with one leg at a time on the machine, with no balance needed.

Easy instructions

  1. Set up as for the leg press with one foot high on the platform.
  2. Lower to about 90 degrees at the knee, press back.
  3. Complete reps on one side, then switch.

Key cues

  • Keep the knee in line with the second toe
  • Both hips stay on the seat

Make it easier: Use both legs.

Make it harder: Pause at the bottom.

Joint note: Same profile as the two-leg press with a lighter weight.

Reverse lunge (Smith machine or rail)

Squat / lungeSmith machine or support
Build

Core idea: Step backward into a lunge with a rail or machine for steadiness. Stepping back is kinder to the knee than stepping forward.

Easy instructions

  1. Stand holding a rail, or with the bar of a Smith machine on your upper back.
  2. Step one foot back and lower until the front thigh is near parallel.
  3. Press through the front foot to return. Switch sides.

Key cues

  • Sit back into the front hip
  • Land softly on the ball of the back foot

Make it easier: Shorter step and depth.

Make it harder: Add dumbbells or slow the lowering.

Joint note: Machine stability reduces balance demand. Check the depth against your knee comfort.

Walking lunge

Squat / lungeOpen space
Build

Core idea: A series of lunging steps along a clear path, like walking with extra effort.

Easy instructions

  1. Step forward into a lunge, lower under control.
  2. Push through the front foot to bring the back leg through into the next step.
  3. Walk along a clear path of 6 to 8 steps each leg.

Key cues

  • Short steady steps
  • Stand tall between steps

Make it easier: Do stationary or supported split squats.

Make it harder: Hold dumbbells at your sides.

Joint note: High balance and knee demand. Not the first choice for knee pain or fall risk.

High-bar squat to parallel

Squat / lungeBarbell, rack
Advance

Core idea: A classic squat with a bar on your upper back, going down until your thighs are level and no further.

Easy instructions

  1. Rest the bar on your upper back, feet shoulder-width apart.
  2. Tighten your tummy, sit down until your thighs are level with the floor, then drive up.
  3. Use a rack with safety bars set just below your lowest rep.

Key cues

  • Tighten your tummy before you go down
  • Knees follow the toes

Make it easier: Goblet squat or leg press.

Make it harder: Pause at the bottom for one second.

Joint note: The bar puts load through your spine, so it is not the first choice if you have low bone density. A leg press is a good swap.

Bulgarian split squat (hand support)

Squat / lungeBench, rail for hand support
Advance

Core idea: A deep one-leg squat with your back foot resting on a bench and a hand on a support.

Easy instructions

  1. Rest the top of one foot on a bench behind you. Hold a support.
  2. Lower straight down, torso leaning slightly forward.
  3. Drive through the front heel to stand.

Key cues

  • Front foot far enough forward that your shin stays almost upright
  • Control the bottom

Make it easier: Supported split squat with the back foot on the floor.

Make it harder: Add a dumbbell in the opposite hand.

Joint note: Deep knee and hip bending under load. Skip it if deep bending causes knee or hip pain.

Hinge

Folding forward at your hips with a long back, then standing tall. It is powerful for the glutes, and the most demanding on your lower back, so build up slowly.

At a glance, by level

Foundation
  • Wall-tap hip hinge
  • Bodyweight good morning
  • Dumbbell Romanian deadlift (RDL)
Build
  • Barbell RDL
  • Cable pull-through
  • 45-degree hyper
  • Single-leg RDL (hand support)
Advance
  • Reverse hyper
  • Good morning (light, controlled)
  • Conventional deadlift (not to failure)

Wall-tap hip hinge

HingeWall
Foundation

Core idea: Learn to fold forward at your hips with a long back by tapping your bottom to a wall.

Easy instructions

  1. Stand one foot-length in front of a wall, soft knees.
  2. Push your hips back to tap the wall, keeping your spine long.
  3. Squeeze your glutes to stand tall.

Key cues

  • Hips back, not down
  • Back long, like a table top

Make it easier: Move closer to the wall.

Make it harder: Hold a light weight against your chest or move to the bodyweight good morning.

Joint note: Good first lesson for the hips and spine.

Bodyweight good morning

HingeNo equipment
Foundation

Core idea: Fold forward at your hips with your hands behind your head, practising the movement before adding weight.

Easy instructions

  1. Place your hands behind your head or across your chest.
  2. Push your hips back and tip your torso forward with a flat back.
  3. Stand tall by squeezing your glutes.

Key cues

  • Soft knees
  • Move at the hip only

Make it easier: Wall-tap hinge.

Make it harder: Light dumbbell RDL.

Joint note: Keep the range pain-free. A little hamstring stretch is normal.

Dumbbell Romanian deadlift (RDL)

HingeDumbbells
Foundation

Core idea: Slide light dumbbells down your thighs by pushing your hips back, then stand tall.

Easy instructions

  1. Hold dumbbells in front of your thighs.
  2. Push your hips back, sliding the weights down your legs, back straight.
  3. When you feel a stretch behind your thighs, squeeze your glutes to stand.

Key cues

  • Dumbbells stay close to your legs
  • Long back, soft knees

Make it easier: Bodyweight good morning.

Make it harder: Barbell RDL or single-leg RDL.

Joint note: Keep to a depth where your back stays flat. Do not chase range.

Barbell RDL

HingeBarbell
Build

Core idea: The same hip-folding movement as the dumbbell version, using a barbell for more load.

Easy instructions

  1. Hold the bar at hip height, shoulder-width grip.
  2. Hinge back and slide the bar down your thighs to below the knee.

Key cues

Make it harder: Slow three-second lowering.

Cable pull-through

HingeCable machine, rope
Build

Core idea: Pull a cable rope through your legs by pushing your hips forward, which keeps your glutes busy and your back calm.

Easy instructions

  1. Face away from a low cable, rope between your legs.
  2. Hinge back, letting the rope travel behind you.
  3. Drive your hips forward to stand tall.

Key cues

  • Finish tall, not leaning back

Make it easier: Lighter stack, shorter range.

Make it harder: Slow lowering and a pause at the top.

Joint note: A kind choice when barbell hinges bother your back.

45-degree hyper

Hinge45-degree hyper bench
Build

Core idea: Fold forward over a padded bench and lift your body back up to a straight line.

Easy instructions

  1. Set the pad just below your hips, feet secured.
  2. Hinge forward from the hips with a long spine.
  3. Raise to a straight line, not beyond.

Conventional deadlift (not to failure)

HingeBarbell
Advance

Core idea: Lift a bar from the floor by pushing the floor away and standing tall, always stopping well before it gets too hard.

Easy instructions

  1. Set the bar over the middle of your feet. Fold forward at the hips, grip the bar and tighten your tummy.
  2. Push the floor away and stand tall.

Key cues

  • Stop with 2 to 3 reps still left in you
  • Reset between every rep

Abduction

Moving your leg out to the side, or turning it outward. It trains the glutes at the side of your hip that keep you steady when you walk or stand on one leg, and it is gentle on recovery.

At a glance, by level

Foundation
  • Side-lying hip raise
  • Clamshell (hip rotation)
  • Banded standing hip abduction
  • Banded seated hip abduction
  • Banded side-stepping
  • Banded or cable kickback
Build
  • Seated hip abduction machine
  • Bridged seated hip abduction
  • Cable hip abduction at a 30-degree diagonal
  • Cable hip external rotation
Advance
  • Glute medius hip thrust (banded)

Side-lying hip raise

AbductionMat
Foundation

Core idea: Lie on your side and lift the top leg out to the side, which trains the glutes at the side of your hip.

Easy instructions

  1. Lie on your side, bottom knee bent for stability, top leg straight and slightly behind your body.
  2. Raise the top leg about 30 to 45 degrees, toes forward.
  3. Lower slowly. Do all reps, then roll over.

Key cues

  • Keep one hip directly above the other
  • Lead with the heel

Make it easier: Use a smaller range or a standing version.

Make it harder: Add an ankle weight or a band above the knees.

Joint note: If the outside of your hip is tender, skip lying on that side. Keep the lifting leg from drifting across your body.

Clamshell (hip rotation)

AbductionMat, mini band optional
Foundation

Core idea: Lie on your side with your knees bent and open the top knee like a clamshell.

Easy instructions

  1. Lie on your side with knees bent about 45 degrees and heels together.
  2. Rotate the top knee up without rolling your pelvis back.
  3. Lower slowly and repeat.

Key cues

  • Small, controlled motion
  • Pelvis stays still

Make it easier: Without a band.

Make it harder: Band above the knees, slower tempo.

Joint note: A gentle starter for hip and knee. Avoid it if side-lying on that hip is painful.

Banded standing hip abduction

AbductionMini band, support
Foundation

Core idea: Stand holding a support and move one leg out to the side against a band.

Easy instructions

  1. Loop a band around both ankles or just above the knees; hold a support.
  2. Move one leg out to the side at a slight diagonal.
  3. Return slowly. Keep the trunk upright.

Key cues

  • Stand tall, do not lean
  • Slight diagonal, as if toward 2 o'clock

Make it easier: Lighter band, smaller range.

Make it harder: Stronger band or hold at the end for two seconds.

Joint note: Your standing leg works to keep you steady, so this doubles as balance practice.

Core idea: Sit on the machine and press the pads apart with your knees, an easy movement to repeat.

Easy instructions

  1. Sit with the pads on the outside of your knees.
  2. Lean slightly forward from the hips and press the pads apart.
  3. Return slowly without letting the weights rest between reps.

Key cues

  • Lean forward from the hips
  • Smooth tempo

Make it easier: Banded seated hip abduction.

Make it harder: Pause for one second at the widest point.

Joint note: Sitting bends your hips a fair amount. Make the movement smaller if you feel a pinch at the front of your hip.

AbductionAbduction machine
Build

Core idea: A variation of the machine press where you lift your hips slightly off the seat while pressing apart.

Easy instructions

  1. Set up on the abduction machine as usual.
  2. Lift your hips slightly off the seat while pressing apart.
  3. Lower and repeat.

Key cues

  • Hips lightly off the seat
  • Control throughout

Make it easier: Standard seated abduction.

Make it harder: Extend the pause.

Joint note: Notice the front of your hip, and make the movement smaller if it pinches.

Cable hip abduction at a 30-degree diagonal

AbductionCable, ankle strap, support
Build

Core idea: Move your leg out and slightly back against a cable while holding a support.

Easy instructions

  1. Attach an ankle strap to a low cable and hold a support.
  2. Move the working leg out and slightly behind you, on a gentle diagonal.
  3. Lower under control. Do all reps, then switch.

Key cues

  • Lead with the heel
  • Hips square

Make it easier: Banded standing abduction.

Make it harder: Pause for two seconds at the top.

Joint note: Use a support. A stable stance matters more than an exact angle.

Cable hip external rotation

AbductionCable or band, bench
Build

Core idea: Sit and turn your thigh outward against a cable or band, keeping your thigh still.

Easy instructions

  1. Sit tall with a cable or band attached at your ankle.
  2. Keep your thigh still and swing your foot inward, which turns your hip outward.
  3. Return slowly.

Key cues

  • Thigh stays still
  • Small, controlled arc

Make it easier: Clamshell.

Make it harder: Slower tempo.

Joint note: Gentle for the knee if the thigh stays still. Stay in a pain-free arc.

AbductionBench, mini band
Advance

Core idea: A hip thrust with a band above your knees, pressing your knees gently outward as you lift.

Easy instructions

  1. Set up a hip thrust with a mini band above the knees.
  2. Press the knees gently outward and drive up.
  3. Hold, then lower slowly.

Key cues

  • Knees out
  • Level hips

Make it easier: Banded glute bridge.

Make it harder: Single-leg version.

Joint note: Same joint profile as the standard hip thrust.

Keep it safe

Safety summary

  • Warm up first, and keep a stable support within reach.
  • Move slowly, breathe out as you push or lift, and stay within a comfortable range.
  • Choose the easier version on a tired day. A lighter session still counts.
  • Discomfort that is mild and settles is fine. Sharp pain is a signal to stop.

Stop signs

Stop the exercise, sit or lie down safely, and contact your clinician if you notice any of these. For chest pain or pressure that does not settle quickly, get emergency medical help.

Chest pain
Dizziness
Unusual breathlessness
Sharp or catching joint pain
Numbness
Pain after a fall

Please read

  • This handout is for education only. It does not replace advice from your clinician.
  • If you have had a hip or knee replacement, follow your surgeon's precautions.
  • If you have low bone density, make spine-conscious choices. The exercises below put more load through your spine, so ask your clinician before you try them.
  • High-bar squat to parallel
  • Dumbbell Romanian deadlift (RDL)
  • Barbell RDL
  • 45-degree hyper
  • Single-leg RDL (hand support)
  • Reverse hyper
  • Good morning (light, controlled)
  • Conventional deadlift (not to failure)
