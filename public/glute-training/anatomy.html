A closer look

Why a level pelvis matters

Try it at home. Stand at a counter, lift one foot, and watch the mirror or ask someone to look from behind. A hip that drops, or a trunk that leans hard to one side, tells you where to focus. Single-leg stance time is also a useful balance measure for Pillar 4.

Joint health

How the glutes protect the joints around them

The glutes do not work alone. They share the job with the hamstrings, adductor magnus, quadriceps, and the deep hip rotators, and they influence the joints above and below.

Hip

The gluteus minimus and medius help hold the ball of the femur centred in its socket, which spreads load across the joint surface.

Knee

When the hip muscles tire, the thigh can drift inward on stairs and squats. Strong hip abductors help keep the knee tracking over the foot.

Low back and pelvis

The gluteus maximus connects with the ligaments and fascia around the sacrum, helping transfer force between your legs and trunk.

Common situations and how to adapt

Pain on the outer hip (gluteal tendon irritation)

Pain over the bony point at the side of the hip, especially when lying on that side, climbing stairs or standing with your weight hung on one hip, often involves the tendons of the gluteus medius and minimus.

  • Avoid crossing the top leg across the midline when side-lying; place a pillow between your knees.
  • Avoid habitually standing with your weight hung on one hip.
  • Start with exercises that load the muscle without compressing the tendon, such as bridges and standing abduction to a small range, and build gradually.
  • Persistent outer-hip pain deserves a clinical assessment before heavy loading.

Hip osteoarthritis or a hip or knee replacement

Strengthening the muscles around an arthritic or replaced joint is generally recommended. Choose ranges that feel comfortable, favour supported exercises such as bridges, leg press and machine abduction, and follow your surgeon’s precautions after joint replacement.

Low bone density or osteoporosis

Hip-focused strengthening is valuable, but the spine needs protection. Prefer bridges, thrusts, leg press, step-ups and cable pull-throughs. Avoid exercises that load a rounded spine, including the rounded-back 45-degree hyper described in the source notes, deep loaded spinal flexion, and deadlifts to failure. The exercise library has a spine-conscious filter.

I lose my balance on one leg

Single-leg work builds the gluteus medius, and a rail or counter lets you train it safely while balance catches up. Keep a hand on the support and reduce it gradually. The balance-demand filter in the library shows which exercises need no support.
