How to progress

Change one lever at a time, and only when the current session feels comfortable.

  1. Reps. Work toward the top of the rep range with good form.
  2. Load. When you reach the top of the range on every set, add the smallest load increase.
  3. Control. Add a pause at the top (the source suggests it for hip thrusts) or slow the lowering.
  4. Complexity. Move from two-leg to one-leg, or from supported to unsupported.

The tracker above tells you when you have hit the top of a range.

Sets and recovery

More is not always better. The source notes that one or two sets per exercise can be enough, and that a plan should be built so drastic deloads are rarely needed. Use the readiness check below to dial a session down instead of skipping it.

Note: the source notes cite about 32 sets a week for the Advance sample; the sets actually listed add up to 35, which is what this tracker counts.

Pillar 3 · Recovery (P3-C8)

How ready are you today?

Excessive soreness and a dip in performance are signs you may be doing too much. Rate how you feel and we will suggest how to shape today’s session.

This is called auto-regulation: adjusting the day rather than following the plan blindly. It tends to protect consistency, which matters more than any single session.

Train with confidence

A simple guide to discomfort

Muscle effort and mild soreness are normal. Joint pain and symptoms that worry you are not. Use this as a quick check during and after each session.

Carry on

  • Muscle burn or fatigue in the glutes and thighs
  • Mild stiffness or soreness up to about 3 or 4 out of 10 that settles within a day

Adjust

  • Joint discomfort of about 5 or 6 out of 10, or soreness that lingers beyond a day or two
  • Reduce the load or range, use support, or swap the exercise

Stop and seek advice

  • Chest pain, dizziness or unusual breathlessness
  • Sharp or catching joint pain, numbness or tingling
  • Pain after a fall or sudden injury

Breathe through effort. Exhale as you lift or stand. Holding your breath on heavy sets can raise blood pressure, which matters if you have a heart or blood-pressure condition.

Start light. The source advises starting light and progressing gradually, especially with riskier exercises such as heavy hinges. This matters even more as we age.
